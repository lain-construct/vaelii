(comment body_part "A part an organism grew — a wing, a heart, a leaf. It stays a body part once transplanted into another organism or shed from its own.")
;; An organism and a part it grew are both biological and never the same thing; nothing
;; says every biological thing is one of the two.  The roster installs each part's genl

;; A leg of lamb or a chicken wing is food and a body part at once.
(orthogonal food body_part)
;; An artifact is anything intentionally made, so an engineered bacterium or an organ grown
;; in a lab is biological and an artifact at once.
(orthogonal biological artifact)

(comment largerThan
         "(largerThan ?kind1 ?kind2) means that a ?kind1 is bigger than a ?kind2. A claim about two KINDS rather than about two things — a type_relation_predicate — so (largerThan dog cat) says dogs are bigger than cats and says nothing whatever about any particular dog. Transitive and asymmetric, so a cycle of size claims is a contradiction the KB reports rather than a chain it walks. Declared transitiveInArgInverse on both positions along genl, which is what carries a claim down to subkinds: a kind of dog is larger than a kind of cat. That distribution is itself a claim about largerThan, which is why it is declared rather than assumed — the same inheritance is false of a chihuahua and a maine coon, and nothing in a sentence says which case it is in.")
(binary_predicate largerThan)
(type_relation_predicate largerThan)
(genlArg largerThan 1 tangible)
(genlArg largerThan 2 tangible)
(transitive largerThan)
(asymmetric largerThan)
(transitiveInArgInverse largerThan 1 genl)
(transitiveInArgInverse largerThan 2 genl)

(comment locatedIn
         "(locatedIn ?object ?place) means that ?object sits within ?place. Declared transitive, so a chain of containments is walked by the closure prover rather than materialized, and a joined rule carries location down parthood — a part is located wherever its whole is. Both arguments are physical objects: a region is CxSpace's business, and partOfRegion is the relation to reach for there.")
(binary_predicate locatedIn)
(instance_relation_predicate locatedIn)
(arg locatedIn 1 tangible)
(arg locatedIn 2 tangible)
(transitive locatedIn)

(comment madeOf
         "(madeOf ?object ?substance) means that ?object is made of ?substance. About one particular object and the stuff it is made of, so it is instance-level; what a whole kind is made of is a claim about that kind. Partial rather than exhaustive — a thing may be made of several substances, and saying it is made of one does not deny the others.")
(binary_predicate madeOf)
(instance_relation_predicate madeOf)
(arg madeOf 1 tangible)
(arg madeOf 2 substance)

(comment partOf
         "(partOf ?part ?whole) means that ?part is a component of ?whole. Declared transitive, so a part of a part is a part and the chain is answered by the closure prover rather than stored. Structural parthood between physical objects — not membership in a collection, and not the spatial containment partOfRegion states of regions.")
(binary_predicate partOf)
(instance_relation_predicate partOf)
(arg partOf 1 tangible)
(arg partOf 2 tangible)
(transitive partOf)

(comment partType
         "(partType ?wholeKind ?partKind) means that a ?wholeKind has a ?partKind among its parts. The type-level reading of partOf: (partType bird wing) says birds have wings, where (partOf LeftWing Pingu) says one thing is part of another. Declared transitiveInArgInverse along genl on the FIRST position only, and the asymmetry is the claim: a kind of bird has whatever parts birds have, so penguins have wings without a word being written about penguins, while the second position preserves nothing — birds having wings says nothing about which kinds of wing they have. Linked to partOf by typeToInstancePred, which records that the two are readings of one relation and deliberately moves no claim between them: going from the kinds to their members needs a quantifier reading (every bird has SOME wing) that nothing here fixes, and guessing it is how a claim about kinds quietly becomes a false claim about individuals.")
(binary_predicate partType)
(type_relation_predicate partType)
(genlArg partType 1 tangible)
(genlArg partType 2 tangible)
(transitiveInArgInverse partType 1 genl)
(typeToInstancePred partType partOf)

;; --- time / time_point / time_interval: time itself --------------------------
(comment time "A time, as in \"at that time\": a moment or a stretch of time as such, not something that happens in it. In time and in no space, and never a cause: the year 2000 broke nothing, two-digit years did, at the rollover. Parent of time_point (a moment) and time_interval (a stretch).")
(genl time temporal)
(genl time aspatial)
(genl time acausal)

(comment time_point "A moment rather than a stretch — one of the two instants bounding an interval. What the point algebra relates, and what startOf and endOf reach from an interval.")

(comment time_interval "A stretch of time as such, e.g. a year, a day, an hour — not something that happens in it. What the calendar constructors YearFn, MonthFn and DayFn name.")

;; A time is a moment or a stretch, never both: an instant has no extent. The partition
;; installs each part's genl edge to time, so neither is stated, and time_point reaches
;; temporal through it.
(set/monotonic (partition time time_point time_interval))
;; A time is not a state of affairs in it.
(set/monotonic (disjoint time situation))

;; --- situation / static_situation / event: the state-of-affairs hierarchy ------
(comment situation "A state of affairs: objects bearing properties or relations over a time interval. Parent of static_situation (the state holds unchanged) and event (the world's state changes); change is the specialization axis.")
(genl situation temporal)

(comment static_situation "A situation extended in time but unchanged, a held state, e.g. a file existing. Contrast event, the kind whose state changes.")
(genl static_situation situation)

(comment event "Something that happens: an occurrence located in time whose state changes. The event-calculus slots (happens/initiates/terminates) are typed temporal and no event collection was declared; this names one.")
(genl event situation)

;; A situation either holds unchanged or changes, never both — the specialization axis
;; is a partition, so the two specializations are disjoint.
(set/monotonic (disjoint static_situation event))

;; --- causal / acausal: whether a thing can occupy a cause slot -----------------
(comment causal "A thing that can be a cause, i.e. can occupy the cause slot of a causal relation. One side of the causal/acausal division of thing.")
(genl causal thing)

(comment acausal "A thing that cannot be a cause: evidence, data, attributes, records. The other side of the causal/acausal division of thing.")
(genl acausal thing)

;; The causal/acausal division: a thing either can be a cause or cannot, so the two
;; sides are disjoint. This also makes causal_event and acausal_event disjoint, by the
;; genl closure of disjointness.
(set/monotonic (disjoint causal acausal))

;; --- causal_event / acausal_event: events by whether they can cause ------------
;; defined via intersection (CxCore); its rules derive the genls and membership.
(comment causal_event "An event that is a cause: the intersection of causal and event.")
(intersection causal_event causal event)

(comment acausal_event "An event that is not a cause (an observation or receipt is one): the intersection of acausal and event.")
(intersection acausal_event acausal event)
